The problem

Manual status decks

Someone spends an afternoon every week assembling a status update from memory, Slack scrollback, and whatever's left in their notes.

Risk registers nobody updates

A spreadsheet gets created at kickoff, filled in once, and never opened again — until the thing it should have caught actually happens.

Or an AI tool that just guesses

Plenty of "AI PM assistants" will happily tell you a project is on track from a prompt and a vibe. When the data doesn't support an answer, that's a problem an LLM should say out loud — not paper over.

How it's different

Every agent separates what happened (computed by plain, auditable code) from how it's explained (written by the model). The rubric decides; the model narrates. A few concrete examples from how the product actually behaves:

Milestone slip

A milestone that's a few days late reads at risk, not a panicked off track — there's a configurable grace period before a slip escalates, because that's how a real PM actually judges a late date.

Sparse data

A project with too little recent activity to classify honestly reports insufficient data — never a guessed on track just because nothing bad was found.

Severity you can trust

Before a communication draft about an open risk can be saved, the model has to correctly restate that risk's real severity. Get it wrong, and the save is rejected — never silently corrected, never sent out misstated.

The agents

Ten specialist agents, each doing one job well. Pick only the ones you need — pricing is a la carte, never a bundle.

Intake

Runs a real conversation with a stakeholder to build a project charter — asking follow-ups on vague answers and flagging contradictions instead of just recording whatever it's told.

Backlog / BA

Decomposes a finalized charter into a prioritized, traceable backlog, and keeps it groomed as new requests come in — with prioritization scored by config, not vibes.

Status

Classifies project health from real task, milestone, and comms signals against an explainable rubric, with every citation traceable back to the data that produced it.

Risk

Surfaces dependency chains, ownerless work, technical-debt language, and reopened tasks before they become a fire drill — tightening automatically as a milestone nears.

Comms

Drafts audience-appropriate updates — internal, client-facing, executive, or escalation — that are guardrailed to never soften or omit a real bad classification.

Portfolio

Rolls every active project up into one leadership view: health, attention-needed ranking, shared-resource conflicts, and cross-project risk clustering.

Task Auto-Update

Reads real evidence in task history and comms to propose field updates — applied automatically for autonomous projects, queued for approval when a dedicated PM wants the final say.

Digest

Compiles a periodic (daily/weekly/monthly) progress summary across tasks, status, risk, and issues — so nobody has to assemble one by hand.

Project Signal

Scans inbound messages — forwarded emails, chat excerpts, meeting notes — for signs someone's asking for a new project, and flags it for a human to review.

Retrospective

On project close, writes a short, specific set of lessons learned — the kind you'd actually tell a new PM inheriting a similar project, not generic platitudes.

Two more agents ship free with every plan: Orchestrator, which routes and sequences the specialists above, and an in-app Help assistant for getting the most out of the product itself.
